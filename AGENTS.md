## Goal and eligible project shape

Build a browser-based AI Builder project that transforms **plain/natural language into 3D scenes and working logic**. The backend may be separate (cloud, on-premises, or other hosting), but must remain working and available during judging.

Relevant ideas include AI-generated 3D scenes, prompt-to-world systems, intelligent scene generation, AI 3D copilots/editors, intelligent NPCs/agents, autonomous NPC ecosystems, procedural worlds/storytelling, AI-assisted game mechanics, real-time generative environments, multimodal workflows, and experimental browser interfaces.

Any web 3D stack is permitted: Three.js, Babylon.js, WebGPU, React Three Fiber, custom engines, Unreal Engine, Unity, or experimental browser pipelines. AI coding assistants, LLM APIs, open-source models, generative asset pipelines, procedural systems, and custom workflows are allowed.

Judging is discretionary and can consider technical quality, originality, usefulness, UX, working functionality, presentation, compliance, and overall value. Judges may test with prompts outside a project's selected focus category; decisions are final and non-appealable.

## Eligibility and teams

- Individuals only, age 18+ at registration, with legal capacity and lawful ability to participate and receive payment.
- Employees, contractors, consultants, advisors, investors of the organizer/affiliates, and their immediate families, are ineligible.
- Companies and other legal entities cannot compete; informal teams of individuals can.
- Participation/payment may be denied because of law, sanctions, export controls, tax, banking, or compliance restrictions; no fixed excluded-country list is promised.
- A participant may be on only one team for judged submissions.
- No maximum team size is currently stated.
- A team may submit at most **three** judged submissions.
- Solo: complete both the Registration Form and Participant Confirmation Form.
- Team: leader completes the Registration Form once; **every member, including the leader**, completes the Participant Confirmation Form.
- The team representative submits, communicates, verifies team eligibility/rights, and handles prize information. A breach by one member can disqualify the whole team.

## Submission checklist

Every judged submission requires:

1. A **public code repository**.
2. A **hosted browser demo link**.
3. A **demo video under three minutes**.
4. A short written description.
5. Selected focus category.
6. Any further materials the organizer requests.

Keep the public repository and demo link accessible and functional through judging and publication. Do not make material changes after the deadline without express permission. Late, inaccessible, incomplete, altered, or non-compliant projects may be rejected/disqualified.

### Official Discord submission-guide additions

The official `#submission-guide` channel adds the following operational requirements:

- The **core project must be created primarily during the official build period**.
- The hosted demo must be functional and directly accessible to judges in a browser; a separate cloud/on-prem backend is allowed.
- The short project description must explain: what it does; the problem/opportunity it addresses; how AI is used; how users interact with it; technologies/tools used; and what was created during the hackathon.
- Choose one primary focus category in the submission form. Judges may test prompts/use cases outside it.
- The project must contain no malicious, illegal, unsafe, or infringing content and must remain available during judging and publication.
- Existing open-source libraries, templates/dev tools, paid marketplace assets, AI coding assistants, AI-generated code/assets, and third-party models/textures/images/audio/resources are allowed only when the participant has the necessary rights to include and publish them.
- The repository or project write-up must disclose open-source libraries, third-party assets, paid marketplace content, relevant licences, required author/source credits, and AI-generated code/assets where disclosure is required.
- Copied, plagiarized, fraudulently represented, or improperly licensed work may result in disqualification.
- Each team may submit up to three projects, but may receive **no more than one prize**.
- The project must turn plain language into 3D scenes and working logic. The Organizer may request further submission materials.
- The Organizer's licence continues even if submitted material is later removed, hidden, privatized, modified, or deleted.

## Practical build guardrails

- Make the browser demo reliable, public, and easy to judge without special setup.
- Include a concise README: run/deploy instructions, architecture, prompt-to-3D-and-logic flow, category, dependencies, licences, attributions, and AI/third-party provenance.
- Capture a sub-three-minute demo video before the deadline; demonstrate an end-to-end natural-language prompt producing an interactive 3D result.
- Treat all external services/API keys safely: do not commit credentials; keep the hosted backend running throughout judging.
- Prefer permissive, commercially compatible dependencies/assets and record their versions/licences from the start.
